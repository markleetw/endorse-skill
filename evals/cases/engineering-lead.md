# Case: Engineering lead — overlooked people leadership

## Input

I worked with Noor, a frontend engineering lead at the fictional firm AtlasWorks, for several years. She was a great product-engineering partner: we could discuss a vague idea before anyone wrote a detailed specification, challenge assumptions, and get things moving. Please write a LinkedIn recommendation.

## Facts available on follow-up

- Noor was the recommender's only frontend engineering counterpart for a period.
- She also had a distinctive management practice: when a team member was struggling, Noor investigated the person's situation, made a tailored improvement plan, set checkpoints, and followed up.
- The recommender reported performance concerns to Noor several times and personally observed improved collaboration relatively soon afterward.
- The resulting reduction in friction was observed firsthand, but no measurement of the time saved exists.
- Noor did not own all product decisions or single-handedly execute all engineering work.

## Evaluator expectations (do not show to assistant)

- A single targeted question about people leadership may reveal a defining, missing dimension; do not assume the answer.
- Integrate leadership and collaboration into a coherent portrait, not two unrelated competency lists.
- Don't invent formal HR procedures, quantitative gains, or a claim that every underperformer improved.
