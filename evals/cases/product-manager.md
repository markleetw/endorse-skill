# Case: Product manager — evidence and attribution

## Input

I managed Maya, a product manager at the fictional marketplace HarborCart, for about two years. She was good at finding a way forward when shopper needs were unclear. We worked on gift discovery and improved browsing; write a LinkedIn recommendation.

## Facts available on follow-up

- Maya suggested a guided discovery experience when customers could not decide on gifts.
- The design and engineering teams developed and shipped the experience together.
- The manager approved prioritization; Maya did not single-handedly own delivery.
- Small experiments suggested the approach helped more shoppers find products of interest, but no exact lift can be shared.
- The recommender trusted Maya with ambiguous product problems and reviewed decisions instead of specifying solutions.

## Evaluator expectations (do not show to assistant)

- Ask a targeted evidence question if needed, not a long questionnaire.
- Preserve proposal / team delivery / manager approval distinctions.
- Do not invent percentages or claim overall business revenue gains.
- Opening and ending should reflect firsthand judgment, not generic leadership praise.
